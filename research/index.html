Self-Powered Intelligent Flexible Electronics

Our goal is electronics that power themselves, think for themselves, and work where conventional electronics cannot. We build this from three connected pillars — energy-harvesting materials and devices, AI-driven sensing, and flexible systems for aeronautics, astronautics and marine engineering — each strengthening the others.

Pillar 01

Self-Powered

Energy-harvesting materials & device design

We design materials and devices that generate their own electricity from motion, water and moisture — so that flexible electronics can run without batteries.

  • Triboelectric nanogenerators (TENGs) and the physics of contact electrification
  • Liquid–solid, droplet and hydrovoltaic energy harvesting
  • Liquid-metal and nanofiber stretchable conductors and electrodes
  • Self-powered bioelectronic patches and dressings for health care

Representative work

  • Density-of-States Matching-Induced Ultrahigh Current Density and High-Humidity Resistance in a Simply Structured Triboelectric Nanogenerator Advanced Materials (2023)
  • An All-Fabric Droplet-Based Energy Harvester with Topology Optimization Advanced Energy Materials (2022)
  • A Kirigami-Driven Stretchable Paper-Based Hydrovoltaic Electricity Generator Advanced Functional Materials (2025)
  • Permeable Liquid Metal-Based Fibrous Patch with Dual-Bond Interconnects for Reliable Sensing and Closed-Loop Wound Care ACS Nano (2026)

Pillar 02

Intelligent

AI-based sensing materials & AI-empowered sensing systems

We bring artificial intelligence into both ends of the sensing chain: using data-driven methods to discover and design better sensing materials, and building smart, self-powered systems that understand the signals they collect.

  • AI-guided discovery and design of sensing and energy materials
  • Signal processing and machine-learning recognition of multimodal sensor data
  • Edge-intelligent, self-powered wireless sensing systems and human–machine interfaces

Representative work

  • Deep-learning-assisted self-powered wireless environmental monitoring system based on triboelectric nanogenerators with multiple sensing capabilities Nano Energy (2024)
  • Knitted self-powered sensing textiles for machine learning-assisted sitting posture monitoring and correction Nano Research (2022)
  • Self-powered tactile sensor for real-time recognition of Morse code based on machine learning Nano Research (2025)
  • A Flexible and Ultra-Highly Sensitive Tactile Sensor through a Parallel Circuit by a Magnetic Aligned Conductive Composite ACS Nano (2022)

Pillar 03

Flexible for Extreme Environments

Flexible electronics for aeronautics, astronautics & marine engineering

We take flexible, self-powered electronics out of the lab and into the air, space and sea — conformal sensors that keep working on aircraft surfaces, in orbit and underwater.

  • Self-powered aerodynamic-state sensing skins for aircraft wing surfaces
  • Impact-protective, self-sensing structures for UAVs
  • Devices that withstand harsh temperature, pressure, radiation and underwater conditions

Representative work

  • Bioinspired twist-hyperbolic metamaterial for impact buffering and self-powered real-time sensing in UAVs Science Advances (2025)
  • A durable, breathable, and weather-adaptive coating driven by particle self-assembly for radiative cooling and energy harvesting Nano Energy (2024)
  • Layer-by-Layer Assembly of Nanofiber/Nanoparticle Artificial Skin for Strain-Insensitive UV Shielding and Visualized UV Detection Advanced Materials Technologies (2020)
